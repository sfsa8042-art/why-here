# Taiwan × Advanced Semiconductor Foundry Manufacturing — Research Scope (Stage 11A)

## Objective
Investigate: **How did Taiwan develop its strength in advanced semiconductor manufacturing?**
Explain the *formation* of Taiwan's specialisation in **dedicated (pure-play) foundry
manufacturing** without assuming the conclusion in advance.

## Public presentation framing
- **Country:** Taiwan
- **Specialisation:** Semiconductor foundry manufacturing
- **Public question:** How did Taiwan develop its strength in semiconductor foundry manufacturing?
- **Plain-language industry explanation:** Semiconductor foundries manufacture chips designed
  by other companies. Building them requires highly specialised factories, engineering
  knowledge, supplier networks and continual investment.
- **Status:** Research planned (public Atlas stays `planned`, `availableModes: []`).

> **Why not "advanced".** The word "advanced" is deliberately absent from the public
> specialisation. This pack documents the *formation* of the dedicated/pure-play foundry model
> and early manufacturing capability; it does **not** yet document the later formation of
> advanced-node manufacturing leadership. "Advanced" may enter the public specialisation only
> when a later evidence pack documents that transition.

The plain-language explanation is documentation only; the Atlas presentation record
(`content/atlas/presentation.ts`) carries the specialisation label and question. No Explore
or Sources action is added to the public Atlas in this stage.

## What the case is (and is not) about
The case concerns **one part of the value chain**: dedicated foundry (contract wafer)
manufacturing, and specifically its *formation* in Taiwan (1970s–late 1980s). It deliberately
distinguishes:
- the broader semiconductor **industry** (design + fabrication + packaging + testing + equipment/materials);
- **design** (fabless / IC-design houses);
- **integrated-device manufacturers (IDMs)** who design *and* fabricate;
- **pure-play foundry** manufacturing (fabricate only, for others);
- **advanced-node** manufacturing (a moving frontier — historically qualified, see TERMINOLOGY.md);
- **packaging & testing** (OSAT);
- **equipment & materials**.

Taiwan is **not** presented as the leader of every part of this chain. Unsupported
superlatives ("the world's semiconductor capital", "dominates the entire industry",
"indispensable to the world") are not used.

## Historical scope (provisional — no forced end date)
1. **Preconditions** before the dedicated semiconductor programme (technocratic policy, education).
2. **Institutional / technology-transfer formation** — ITRI (1973), ERSO (1974), the RCA
   agreement (1976) and demonstration fab (1977).
3. **Early domestic manufacturing** — UMC (1980); Hsinchu Science Park (1980).
4. **Foundry-model creation** — Chang → ITRI (1985); Philips VLSI demo fab (1986); TSMC (1987).
5. **Initial ecosystem formation** — spin-offs, public→private transfer, returnee engineers (early 1990s).

Present-day statistics are kept **out** of historical claims; any present-day context must be
separately and currently sourced (not authored in this pack).
