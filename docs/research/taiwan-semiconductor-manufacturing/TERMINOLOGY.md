# Taiwan × Advanced Semiconductor Foundry Manufacturing — Terminology (Stage 11A)

Precise definitions so the case does not present Taiwan as leader of every part of the chain,
and does not use present-day technology terms for earlier periods.

## The value chain (distinct parts)
- **Semiconductor industry** — the whole chain: design + fabrication + packaging + testing +
  equipment/materials. Taiwan is *not* claimed to lead all of it.
- **IC design (fabless)** — firms that design integrated circuits but own no fab; they
  commission manufacturing. (e.g. later Taiwanese design houses.)
- **Integrated device manufacturer (IDM)** — a firm that **both designs and fabricates** its
  own chips (e.g. Intel, TI, Motorola historically). The IDM was "the conventional model"
  before the foundry (Chang oral history, p.12–13).
- **Foundry** — a factory that **fabricates wafers for others**. A **pure-play / dedicated
  foundry** does *only* contract fabrication and sells no IC products of its own design
  (TSMC's model; TSMC 20-F: "a dedicated foundry"). Contrast an IDM that also takes some
  outside foundry work.
- **Wafer fabrication ("fab")** — turning blank silicon wafers into patterned dies; the
  most capital-intensive step. Wafer size marks eras here: **3-inch** (RCA transfer, 1976–77),
  **4-inch** (UMC), **6-inch** (VLSI demo fab / early TSMC).
- **Packaging & testing (OSAT)** — assembling dies into packages and testing them; a *separate*
  sub-sector, **not** covered by this pack's claims (gap G6).
- **Equipment & materials** — tools (lithography, etch, deposition) and materials (wafers,
  chemicals, masks); a *separate* sub-sector, not covered here.

## "Advanced manufacturing" — historically qualified
"Advanced" is relative to its period. In 1976 the RCA process was **7-micron CMOS**; Chang's
oral history states that by 1987 ITRI's RCA-derived technology was "two and a half generations
behind the leading level." **Do not** describe 1970s–80s Taiwan manufacturing using present-day
node terms (e.g. "7-nanometre", "advanced-node") — those belong only to a separately-sourced
present-day context section, not to historical claims.

## Which part this case actually concerns
The Taiwan case explains the formation of **dedicated (pure-play) foundry manufacturing** —
one part of the chain — and its institutional/technology-transfer origins, not the whole
semiconductor industry. The **public specialisation label is "Semiconductor foundry
manufacturing"** (no "advanced"): the evidence documents the *formation* of the foundry model,
not the later formation of advanced-node leadership. Per the section above, "advanced" belongs
only to a separately-sourced present-day context, not to this pack — and not to the public label
until a later pack documents the advanced-manufacturing transition.

## Phrases deliberately avoided
"the undisputed global leader", "the world's semiconductor capital", "dominates the entire
semiconductor industry", "indispensable to the world" — none is used unless an exact,
independently-supported, appropriately-qualified claim warrants it (none does in this pack).
